Start with the calendar

Good morning. Before any put, covered call, or wheel discussion, look at the week. Inflation prints and earnings releases change option prices through implied volatility. If you skip the calendar, you leave those changes out of the decision.

This letter is education from EarningsWheel.com. The goal is clear decisions and clear risks. No promised income. When a premium is shown as a paper-trade illustration, it is labeled that way and is not a fill. When a bid and ask come from a public chain, they are delayed quotes, not an order ticket.

Paper-trade cash-secured put (educational only, not a fill): sell one Nov 13 $95 put. Cash to secure is $9,500. Using a midpoint illustration of $2.55 between the $2.22–$2.88 bid-ask, premium would be $255, or 2.68% of the $9,500 cash for the short holding period before fees. Breakeven if assigned would be $92.45. If the stock went to zero after assignment, the loss would be about $9,245 after that premium. Assignment can occur at expiration; early assignment is also possible. Simplified math excludes commissions, fees, taxes, and price improvement.

Paper-trade covered call (educational only): if you already owned 100 shares near the $98.65 close ($9,865 stock cost), selling the Nov 13 $100 call at a midpoint illustration of $3.825 between the $3.65–$4.00 bid-ask would collect about $383, or 3.88% of that stock cost for the holding period before fees. Breakeven on the shares after the premium would be about $94.83. Upside is capped near $100 plus the premium if called away. These percentages are not annualized.

Hypothetical arithmetic only

The midpoints above are arithmetic between published bid and ask. They are not an executed trade, not Chuck's order, and not a promise the midpoint was available. Use your broker's live chain before acting.

One risk rule

Never sell a cash-secured put unless you are ready to own 100 shares at the strike with the cash already reserved. On an inflation and earnings week, assume gaps can be larger than quiet sessions. If one assignment would force you to scramble, the put is too big.
