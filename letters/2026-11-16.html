Last week, and what matters now

What a week. Nvidia lost its CEO on Thursday, and the market still put up its best week since August. The S&P 500 gained 2.5% to close Friday at 7,561.67, the Nasdaq added 2.3% to 25,136.27, and the Dow rose 2.1% to 52,347.55. That broke a four-week losing streak for the S&P and Nasdaq. Nvidia itself finished the week down about 13%, but money rotated into other tech names instead of leaving the market.

Two things kept it from being a clean rally. October producer prices rose 0.3% for the month, but 6.5% from a year earlier, the biggest annual jump since November 2022. And traders are now pricing a better chance that the Fed raises rates in December. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, closed Friday at 19.30, up from Thursday.

This week the AI trade faces a real test: Nvidia reports Tuesday night, and Anthropic is expected to start trading Thursday under the ticker ANT. Add Walmart, Target and Home Depot, and we'll learn a lot about both tech and the American shopper.

What it means for selling premium: Retail sales on Tuesday morning, Nvidia that night and the Fed minutes Wednesday can all move the whole market, not just one stock. With the VIX near 19, premiums are healthy, but that's because the market sees real risk. If you sell puts this week, size them as if the stock could move the full implied move against you. The short Thanksgiving week that follows gives you one less trading day, so time decay won't fully make up for it.

Setup ideas for education

These are examples for learning, not recommendations. Prices are mid quotes from Friday's close and will be different when the market opens. Premiums are per share; one contract covers 100 shares. Figures are before commissions.

1. Cash-secured put on Nvidia through earnings
Sell the NVDA Nov 18 $162.50 put for about $6.85.

  • Cash set aside: $16,250. Premium: $685, or about 4.2% of the capital, for three trading days.
  • Breakeven: $155.65.
  • Main risk: This strike is at the money, so it's aggressive. If Nvidia falls the full ±8.6% implied move, to about $149, you'd own 100 shares at $162.50 that are already worth about $1,350 less than you paid (roughly $665 after the premium). A strike below the expected range would pay less and be safer.

2. Cash-secured put on Walmart, the first spin of the wheel
Sell the WMT Nov 20 $128 put for about $3.92.

  • Cash set aside: $12,800. Premium: $392, or about 3.1%.
  • Breakeven: $124.08.
  • Main risk: Walmart fell 9.2% and 7.3% after its last two reports. If you're assigned, you own a defensive name at an effective $124.08, and the next step is idea #3.

3. Covered call on Walmart, the other half of the wheel
Own 100 WMT shares (Friday close $128.09) and sell the Nov 20 $128 call for about $4.08.

  • Premium: $408, or about 3.2% of the $12,809 share value.
  • Downside breakeven: $124.01. If the shares are called away at $128, the most you can make is about $3.99 a share.
  • Main risk: The premium only cushions the first $4 of a drop, and on a big beat you give up everything above $128.

The lesson: Earnings premium is fat because the risk is real. The implied move tells you how far the market thinks the stock could go. Choosing a strike outside that range is usually the difference between getting paid for patience and getting paid to take a hit.

Scorecard

This is the first Earnings Wheel letter with setup ideas, so there's nothing to grade yet. Next week we'll check how each of these three examples would have played out, wins and losses both.

Risk reminder

A cash-secured put means you have to buy the shares at the strike, even if the stock gaps far below it overnight. A covered call caps your upside but leaves you with nearly all the downside. Earnings gaps happen before you can react. Don't sell more contracts than you would gladly own, and keep enough cash to cover every put you sell.
